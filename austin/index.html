Heating and cooling failures rarely wait for a convenient day, which is why we offer fast, flexible scheduling for most situations. The urgent calls we run tend to follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. And any time of year, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, plugged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have quit. Because our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these get fixed in one visit, at a price you approve before we start.

A few minutes of caution before we arrive can head off a much bigger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be turned off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us right away.

How Austin Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like higher energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of summer.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A capacitor that's starting to fail is an easy, low-cost part to replace, yet the same part, neglected through a full season, can ruin the compressor and force a full system replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so you can reach us fast as soon as something seems off in your home. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
